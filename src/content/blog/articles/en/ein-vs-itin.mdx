An EIN identifies a business for federal tax administration, while an ITIN identifies an individual for certain federal tax purposes. Knowing which number applies helps you prepare the right paperwork for your company.

## What is the difference?

An **EIN** identifies a business for federal tax purposes. An **ITIN** is a personal tax identification number for someone who needs one for a federal tax purpose and is not eligible for an SSN. One is not a replacement for the other. See the [IRS explanation of ITINs](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc857).

## Can you get an EIN without an SSN or ITIN?

It can be possible. The IRS Form SS-4 instructions provide an option for a responsible party who has neither number and is ineligible to obtain one. That exception must fit the actual applicant; it is not a reason to omit a number you already have. Check [line 7b in the official instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iss4).

## Does living in the United States make the application different?

The IRS online tool requires a U.S. or U.S. territory principal place of business and the responsible party’s SSN or ITIN, among other conditions. Applicants outside its criteria must use an appropriate alternative method. Citizenship alone does not determine eligibility for the online tool. The [IRS application page](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number) explains the current conditions.
