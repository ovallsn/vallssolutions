An LLC’s tax classification and any valid tax election can affect how business income is reported and which obligations apply. Some owners explore tax elections as part of business planning; the result depends on ownership, activity, income and tax residence.

## An LLC does not have one fixed tax classification

An LLC is a legal structure created under state law. For federal tax purposes, the IRS classifies it based on the number of owners and any elections it files. A domestic single-member LLC is generally treated as an entity disregarded from its owner unless it elects a different classification. A domestic LLC with two or more members is generally treated as a partnership unless it elects corporate treatment.

That is why forming an LLC and choosing its tax treatment are related but separate decisions. Each election changes filing and compliance duties; there is no option that costs less for every business.

## What a U.S. owner should consider

If a single-member LLC does not elect corporate treatment, its activities are generally reported on the owner’s federal tax return. When the owner is an individual operating a trade or business, the IRS says net earnings may be subject to self-employment tax in the same way as a sole proprietorship.

Some businesses consider different tax elections. Before making one, compare the full effect, including taxes, payroll, bookkeeping, extra forms and ongoing costs. An election that changes one tax in one situation may not be worthwhile in another, and eligibility requirements may apply.

## What changes for an international founder

Tax residence and where work is performed matter. The IRS explains that a nonresident alien is generally taxed in the United States on certain U.S.-source income and income effectively connected with a U.S. trade or business. The owner’s country of residence may also apply its own rules to income, controlled companies or reporting.

Information reporting may apply even when a company does not owe federal income tax in a particular situation. For example, certain foreign-owned U.S. single-member LLCs may need to file Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 when they have reportable transactions. The details and exceptions depend on the facts and the IRS instructions in force.

> **“Wyoming has no state income tax” does not mean “my LLC pays no tax.”** Federal taxes, obligations in another state or the tax rules of the owner’s home country may still apply.

## How to review potential tax savings

- Identify each owner and their tax residence.

- Map where services are performed, contracts are signed, staff are hired and income is earned.

- Compare the default classification with any tax election a qualified professional considers available.

- Include returns, payroll, bookkeeping, the Annual Report and additional state registrations in your calculations.

- Get coordinated advice in the U.S. and your country of residence if both tax systems may apply.
