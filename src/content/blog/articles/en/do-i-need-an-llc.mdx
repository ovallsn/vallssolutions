An LLC gives a business a formal legal structure under state law. Freelancers, agencies, online sellers and independent founders often use one to organize contracts, business records and company finances under a dedicated entity.

## What does an LLC give a business?

An LLC is a company created by filing with a state. It can give a business its own legal identity, set out who owns and manages it, and create a structure for opening business accounts, signing agreements and keeping company records.

The SBA describes LLCs as a structure that can separate business and personal liability. That protection has limits and depends on applicable law and how the business is run, so keep company funds, records and agreements properly organized.

## Why do founders create a U.S. LLC?

- **A clear business structure:** Form an entity for an agency, consulting practice, online business or other operating company.
- **Business-ready records:** Use the company name on contracts, invoices and business paperwork when appropriate.
- **A separate business identity:** Build a foundation for company banking and service-provider applications.
- **Flexible federal tax classification:** The IRS generally classifies a domestic LLC based on its number of members, with certain elections available. The right treatment depends on the facts.

An LLC does not automatically lower a founder’s taxes. A tax classification or election may change what a business owes and which filings it must make, so compare the full effect with a qualified tax professional.

## Can a non-U.S. resident own an LLC?

Many international founders can own a U.S. LLC without living in the United States. An ITIN is not required simply to form an LLC. EIN application details and tax obligations depend on the responsible party, the business and the owner’s tax residence.

## What does it take to keep an LLC organized?

After formation, maintain a Registered Agent, keep company and personal records distinct, track the filing calendar and check tax and licensing obligations where the business operates. For a Wyoming LLC, this includes the state Annual Report and annual license tax.
