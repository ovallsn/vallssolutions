If you own a business and are thinking about spending time in Thailand, separate two questions: how to document self-employment and whether you meet the requirements for a visa category. A U.S. LLC may be part of the records for a genuine business, but **owning one does not grant a visa or guarantee approval**.

## Can a business support a Destination Thailand Visa application?

The Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) includes a Workcation category for digital nomads, remote workers, foreign talent and freelancers. The exact documents depend on the Thai embassy or consulate handling the application.

For example, the Royal Thai Consulate-General in Los Angeles lists an employment contract, employment certificate or professional portfolio as evidence of workcation activity. For self-employed applicants, it also lists a business license or business registration showing the applicant’s name. The Royal Thai Embassy in Budapest lists a cover letter, certificate of incorporation and corporate tax return for business owners.

That shows company records **may be relevant evidence of business activity** when the checklist for your application asks for them. It does not mean a newly formed LLC is enough by itself. Authorities may also request evidence of actual activity and income, financial documents, proof of residence and other requirements. The office handling your case may ask for more.

Do not assume a U.S. LLC authorizes you to work for Thai companies or serve local Thai clients. Check the rules for the specific visa category and any work restrictions with the Thai authorities.

## Other visa categories have different requirements

The two-year Smart “S” category is for entrepreneurs with technology startups established in Thailand and certified in targeted industries. Its published criteria include owning at least 25% of the Thai company or serving as its director, holding a deposit of at least THB 600,000 for three months, and having health insurance. A Wyoming LLC does not replace that Thai company or meet those criteria on its own.

Thailand’s LTR Work-from-Thailand Professional category is designed for remote employees of overseas companies that meet specific size, operating history and revenue criteria, along with personal requirements. Owning a small LLC is not automatically the same as having the employment or qualifying employer required by that category.

## Where a U.S. LLC may fit

If you already operate a real business, company and financial records may help explain your work when an application asks for self-employment evidence. The LLC is only one part of that file. It does not replace visa requirements, prove sufficient income by itself or ensure a favorable decision.

Before forming a company for a possible immigration benefit, confirm with the embassy or consulate that would handle your case which category fits, what evidence it accepts and what work restrictions apply. Checklists can differ by jurisdiction and change over time.
