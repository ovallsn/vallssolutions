Forming a U.S. LLC may make sense when there is a concrete business reason: selling in the U.S. market, contracting with American companies, separating a line of business or structuring an operation that already has ties to the country. You do not need to form a U.S. company just because the internet presents it as a shortcut.

## What an LLC can offer

An LLC is an entity created under a state’s law. It can help separate business activity from personal affairs and set rules for ownership and management. Liability protection has limits: mixing funds, acting improperly, giving personal guarantees or breaking the law may expose owners. The LLC should have accounts, contracts and records that match a real business.

For federal tax purposes, the IRS does not assign every LLC the same treatment. Depending on its number of owners and any tax elections, it may be treated as disregarded from its owner, as a partnership or as a corporation. The legal structure and tax classification are related questions, but they are not the same thing.

## Can it work for an international founder?

In many states, someone can own a U.S. LLC without living in the United States. The [IRS explains that most states do not restrict who may be an LLC member](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/limited-liability-company-llc), though specific rules vary. That does not remove EIN requirements or obligations in the owner’s country. The information needed depends on who the responsible party is and how the IRS application is completed. You do not need an ITIN simply to register an LLC, although a professional can determine whether one is needed for a separate tax matter.

If the business operates from another country, check how that country treats income, effective management, controlled companies and reporting obligations. A U.S. LLC does not automatically replace the structure you need in your home country.

## What company formation does not include

- **An approved bank account.** The LLC may apply, but each financial provider reviews the company, owners, address, activity and country of residence.

- **A visa or permission to work in the United States.** Forming a company does not grant immigration status.

- **Freedom from taxes or filings.** Obligations depend on the business activity, tax classification, owners and relevant jurisdictions.

- **A physical office.** A mailing or Registered Agent address serves a different purpose from proving where a business operates.

> **Start by defining what you need the entity to do.** If the answer is “have an American LLC,” ask one more practical question: what real connection will the company have to the United States?

## Before choosing a state

Compare the state where you live or operate with any alternative you are considering. If a company formed elsewhere conducts business in your state, it may need to register there as a foreign entity and file additional reports. The SBA recommends reviewing where the business operates and which registrations apply.

Before starting, clarify who will own the company, how it will be managed, where it will work, which licenses it needs and who will handle accounting and taxes. Then compare the state filing fee, Registered Agent, Annual Report and the services you actually need.
